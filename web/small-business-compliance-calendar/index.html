Small business compliance calendar: the dates that apply to you, and a yearly template

A small business compliance calendar is a month-by-month list of every filing, payment, posting and renewal your business owes in the coming year, with the rule behind each date so you can check it. For a United States business the federal rows are fixed and listed below with the agency's own wording; the state rows (annual report, sales tax, licenses) you add from your state's pages. The quickest way to build yours: answer the four questions in the next section to find out which rows apply to you, copy the yearly template, delete the rows that do not, and add your state dates. One thing older calendars still list and you can take off: the beneficial ownership (BOI) report, which U.S. companies no longer file (see the questions at the end).

Which rows apply to you: four questions

Most of a compliance calendar depends on four facts about the business. Decide them first, then the template nearly fills itself.

  • Entity type. Sole proprietor or single-member LLC: the business income goes on your Form 1040, due April 15. Partnership or multi-member LLC: Form 1065 by March 15. S corporation: Form 1120-S by March 15. C corporation: Form 1120 by April 15, with its own estimated tax dates (IRS Publication 509, Tax Calendars).
  • Do you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax (individuals, including sole proprietors, partners and S corporation shareholders) or $500 or more (corporations)? If so, the quarterly estimated tax rows apply (IRS, Estimated taxes).
  • Do you have employees? If yes, the payroll rows apply: Form 941 each quarter, Form 940 and Forms W-2 in January, and your deposit schedule (IRS, Employment tax due dates). If you paid any non-employee $2,000 or more during the year (the threshold rose from $600 for tax years beginning after 2025; IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC), Form 1099-NEC is due January 31 whether or not you have employees.
  • How many employees, at the most, during the year? More than 10 brings OSHA's injury and illness log and the posted annual summary, unless your industry is exempt (OSHA, Recordkeeping). 20 or more in a listed industry, or 250 or more anywhere, adds an electronic submission (29 CFR 1904.41). 50 or more full-time or full-time-equivalent employees makes you an applicable large employer with Forms 1094-C and 1095-C (IRS, Information reporting by ALEs).

The federal dates and the rule behind each

Each row gives the rule as the agency states it and the date it produces for a calendar-year business. The IRS moves a date that falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday to the next business day (Publication 509 gives the 2025 W-2 copies as due "by February 2, 2026", because January 31 was a Saturday). The PDF calendars in each year's Publication 509 already include that adjustment, so take next year's exact dates from the 2027 edition when it is published, usually in December.

Print it with the browser's print command: the header and footer are hidden on paper. The reminder dates in the template follow the 30, 14, 7 and 1 day rule below, counted back from the rule date, and are not adjusted for weekends.

How far ahead to set each reminder

A single reminder on the day is too late for anything that needs a document, a login or a payment. Use four, counted back from the due date: 30 days (gather the figures, find the portal login), 14 days (prepare or hand to your accountant), 7 days (file or pay if ready), 1 day (confirm it went through and store the confirmation). For a renewal whose window opens earlier than 30 days out, the business license renewal tracker page explains why the first reminder should land the day the window opens.

What goes in the state rows

Federal dates are the same everywhere; state dates are not, which is why the template leaves them blank rather than guessing. The rows most small businesses need are the annual report or statement of information, a franchise or privilege tax, sales tax returns on a monthly, quarterly or annual cycle set by the state, the state unemployment insurance return, workers' compensation renewal, and business license renewals. Verified examples with the agencies' own pages, including Delaware's March 1 franchise tax, Texas's May 15 report, Oregon's anniversary-date renewal and California's six-month statement window, are on the renewal tracker page. For which licenses your business must hold at all, start with your Secretary of State and the SBA's licenses and permits guide.

When this page does not apply

  • If your business has a fiscal year that is not the calendar year, the return and estimated tax dates shift with it; Publication 509 gives the month-based rules.
  • If you are outside the United States, the federal rows do not apply. Australian businesses have the ATO's key dates for employers; the template still works with those dates in it.
  • If you have already missed a date, go to the agency's own page for penalties and late filing rather than this calendar.
  • Anything that turns on your entity election, your industry's exemption status under OSHA appendix A, or a notice an agency sent you is a question for a CPA, an enrolled agent or an attorney. This page is organizational guidance, not tax or legal advice.

Questions

Do I still need to file a beneficial ownership (BOI) report?

Not if your company was formed in the United States. FinCEN's interim final rule of March 26, 2025 exempted domestic reporting companies, and a final rule issued August 11, 2026 and effective August 14, 2026 made the exemption permanent (FinCEN, Beneficial Ownership Information). Older compliance calendars still list it; take it off yours.

What if a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday?

For federal tax deadlines the IRS says the action is on time if done by the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday (Pub. 509). Its PDF calendars already reflect that: the 2026 edition gives the W-2 copies for 2025 as due February 2, 2026. Copy next year's dates from the 2027 edition when it is published.

Which OSHA dates apply to a small business?

With more than 10 employees in a non-exempt industry, you keep the 300 log and post the Form 300A summary from February 1 to April 30 (29 CFR 1904.32). Electronic submission by March 2 applies only to establishments with 20 to 249 employees in an appendix A industry, or 250 or more employees (29 CFR 1904.41). Every employer must report a death within 8 hours and a hospitalization, amputation or eye loss within 24 hours (OSHA).

Where do state deadlines go?

As rows under the month they fall in. The usual ones are the annual report, franchise tax, sales tax returns, the state unemployment return and license renewals; the renewal tracker page has verified examples and the columns to record for each.
