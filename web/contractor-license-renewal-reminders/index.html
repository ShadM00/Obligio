Contractor license renewal: when the board writes, what late costs, and a reminder plan that covers the bond and insurance too

A contractor license renews on a fixed cycle, usually every two years, and the board's own notice arrives late: about 60 days before expiry in California, as little as 30 days in Texas. Renewing late costs half as much again in both states, and work done while the license is expired counts as unlicensed. So set your own reminders at 90, 60, 45, 30 and 7 days before the date, and set separate ones for the bond and insurance, because a cancelled bond can suspend the license in the middle of the cycle. The table below has the rules for four states, read on the boards' own pages on 18 November 2026.

What a late renewal costs, worked out

The late fee is the small part. In California a sole owner pays $675 instead of $450, which is $225 more. A Texas electrical contractor pays $165 instead of $110 in the first 90 days, $55 more, and $220 after that, double. Texas sets those multiples in law: 1½ times the fee up to 90 days, twice the fee after (Occupations Code §51.401).

The larger cost is the gap. The CSLB says that when you renew late "there will be a break in licensing time and any work performed while the license is expired is considered to be unlicensed". Texas law says a person whose license has expired "may not engage in activities that require a license until the license has been renewed". California has one cushion: since 2021 the registrar must retroactively reinstate an expired license if a completed renewal and the fees arrive within 90 days of expiry. The delinquent fee still applies, and an incomplete form does not count, so it is a backstop, not a plan. What an expired license does to contracts and insurance is covered in what happens if a business license expires.

The license date is not the only date

A renewal can be refused, or a current license suspended, because of something else that ran out. Each of these needs its own reminder.

  • The bond. In Washington a registration "shall be automatically suspended on the effective date" of a bond cancellation (RCW 18.27.060). Washington bonds are $30,000 for general and $15,000 for specialty contractors.
  • Liability insurance. Washington asks for a certificate of at least $250,000. Texas electrical contractors must hold $300,000 per occurrence and $600,000 aggregate, and the business name on the certificate must match the application.
  • Workers' compensation. An active California license must keep its bonds, workers' compensation cover and qualifying individual in place; the CSLB excuses only a license renewed as inactive.
  • Your qualifier. A Texas electrical contractor must employ a licensed master electrician or hold that license. If that person leaves, you must tell TDLR and name another. Their own license has its own expiry.
  • The company's standing. The CSLB checks a corporation's status with the Secretary of State before renewing and will not renew one that is suspended there.
  • Continuing education. Oregon residential contractors "cannot renew a license until the CCB has proof they met the continuing education requirements": 3 hours of laws and business practices plus 5 or 13 further hours each two-year period, depending on years licensed.
  • Your address. Notices go to the last address on record. Texas law says so in terms; the CSLB asks you to report any change.

A five-reminder plan built on the board's own dates

Count back from the expiry date printed on your license or pocket card.

When this page does not apply

  • Your license has already expired. Stop taking licensed work and call the board. Then read what happens if a business license expires.
  • Your state or trade is not in the table. Use your board's own renewal page. The reminder plan still works; change the 60- and 45-day lines to match its notice.
  • The business changed structure or owner. In California a change from sole owner to corporation means a new license, not a renewal. Ask the board before the renewal date.
  • You hold a city or county business license as well. That is a separate renewal; see the business license renewal tracker.
  • A discipline, suspension or criminal-history question is involved. Ask the board or a licensing attorney.

Questions

How long before it expires can I renew a contractor license?

It depends on the board. California's CSLB sends the renewal application about 60 days before expiry and says a renewal must be received before the expiration date to avoid unlicensed time. Texas law requires the licensing department to send written notice at least 30 days before expiry. Check your own board's renewal page for its window.

Can I keep working while my contractor license renewal is late?

Generally no. The CSLB says work performed while a license is expired is considered unlicensed. Texas Occupations Code section 51.401 says a person whose license has expired may not engage in activities that require a license until it is renewed. Ask your board before taking or continuing a job.

What besides the license itself can stop me working?

A cancelled bond or insurance policy. Washington law suspends a contractor's registration automatically on the date the bond is cancelled, and Washington's Department of Labor and Industries says it suspends a registration when a bond or insurance cancels or expires. Track those dates alongside the license date.
