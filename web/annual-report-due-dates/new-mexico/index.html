STATE FILING · NEW MEXICO

New Mexico annual report due date

Corporate Report (Corporate Reports Act, NMSA 1978 § 53-5-2) and supplemental report: First report within 30 days after the certificate of incorporation or certificate of authority is issued; then biennially, on or before the 15th day of the fourth month after the end of the corporation's taxable year. A supplemental report is due within 30 days of a change in registered office/agent, directors or officers, or principal place of business.

From the official NMOneSource (NM Compilation Commission) page, read on 20 November 2026. Your own date depends on when and how your business was formed.

Who has to file the New Mexico Corporate Report (Corporate Reports Act, NMSA 1978 § 53-5-2) and supplemental report?

Domestic and foreign corporations that are not exempted (exemptions listed in § 53-5-4).

The official page says: “a domestic or foreign corporation that is not exempted shall file” (NMOneSource (NM Compilation Commission) — NMSA 1978 Chapter 53 Corporations, Article 5 Corporate Reports (full-chapter document))

When is the New Mexico annual report due?

First report within 30 days after the certificate of incorporation or certificate of authority is issued; then biennially, on or before the 15th day of the fourth month after the end of the corporation's taxable year. A supplemental report is due within 30 days of a change in registered office/agent, directors or officers, or principal place of business. It is filed every two years.

The official page says: “biennially thereafter on or before the fifteenth day of the fourth month” (NMOneSource (NM Compilation Commission) — NMSA 1978 Chapter 53 Corporations, Article 5 Corporate Reports (full-chapter document))

How much does it cost to file in New Mexico?

The state's page we read does not state the fee. The state shows it when you file.

What happens if the New Mexico report is late?

$200 civil penalty for a late report, in addition to the filing fee, payable when the report is filed (§ 53-5-7). Sixty days after written notice of failure to file is mailed, a domestic corporation's certificate of incorporation (or a foreign corporation's certificate of authority) is cancelled unless the report is filed and all fees and penalties are paid in that period; cancelled corporations are stricken from the files.

The official page says: “shall incur a civil penalty of two hundred dollars ($200)” (NMOneSource (NM Compilation Commission) — NMSA 1978 Chapter 53 Corporations, Article 5 Corporate Reports (full-chapter document))
