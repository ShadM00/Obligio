What happens if a business license expires, and what to do today

When a business license expires, three things usually follow, in this order. First, the renewal gets more expensive: a late or delinquent fee is added the day after the printed date (Washington, California's cosmetology board) or a penalty plus monthly interest starts (Delaware, Texas). Second, if the license is the permission to do the work, the work you do while it is expired counts as unlicensed, and the agency can discipline you for it. Third, after a cutoff, which runs from 45 days to five years depending on the agency, you can no longer renew at all: the license is cancelled or the entity forfeited, and you reapply or reinstate from scratch. Renew today if the portal still lets you, and read your agency's own rule in the table below. The examples are American; Australians spell it licence, and the address keeps that spelling so both can find this page.

Can you keep working? A rule to apply in the first ten minutes

Sort your expired item into one of three kinds, because the right first move differs:

  • Permission to do the work (trade, professional, contractor, certification such as EPA lead-safe): stop that work until the renewal is accepted. The CSLB and EPA rules above are explicit that work in the gap is unlicensed or not permitted. Finish nothing on site today that the license covers.
  • Entity registration or tax filing (annual report, statement of information, franchise tax): the business keeps trading, but every day adds penalty or interest, and the real risk is the cutoff: forfeiture, suspension or a void entity. In Texas that means losing the right to sue or defend and personal liability for officers. File and pay within the notice period, today if the portal allows.
  • General business license or permit (state or city business license, sales tax permit, DBA): pay the renewal and late penalty now. Then read the agency's page for a second consequence; where the page is silent, as Washington's FAQ is, phone and write down what you were told, with the date and the name.

If a renewal notice, a notice of intent to forfeit, or any letter with a deadline has arrived, that letter's date overrides everything above: it is the agency telling you how long you have.

What to do today, in order

  1. Find the document and the number. The license certificate, the policy, or the last filing confirmation. The expiry date printed on it is the one the agency works from.
  2. Check the status on the agency's portal, not from memory. Boards publish the status (Current, Delinquent, Canceled); comptrollers publish account status. Save a screenshot with today's date.
  3. Renew in the portal if it still lets you. Pay the delinquent or late fee with the renewal; it cannot be waived by asking, as the CSLB says even of a granted petition.
  4. If the portal refuses the renewal, you are past the cutoff. Look for the words reapply, reinstate, original application or tax clearance letter on the agency's page, and start that process the same day. Keep every receipt.
  5. If a petition or cure window exists, calendar its end date now. The CSLB's 90 days and Texas's 45 days are counted from the expiry date or the mailing of the notice, not from the day you noticed.
  6. Record the lapse in the table below and attach the new certificate when it arrives. The record is what you show a client, an insurer or an auditor who asks about the gap.
  7. Fix the cause. Most lapses have one of four causes: no reminder set, the reminder on a phone that was replaced, the agency's notice sent to an old address, or the person who renewed last time has left. Change the one that applies before you close the file.

When this page does not apply

  • The license was suspended or revoked, not expired. That is a disciplinary matter with its own process (the cosmetology board lists Suspension and Revoked as separate statuses from Delinquent). Speak to the board and, for anything beyond a fee, a licensed attorney.
  • You received a notice with a deadline. Follow the notice. If it names a court, a hearing or personal liability, as a Texas forfeiture notice can, get an attorney or CPA involved before the window closes.
  • You are in Australia. The Australian Business Licence and Information Service lists licences by location and activity, and company annual review fees are set by ASIC. The steps above still work; the examples do not.
  • It is an insurance policy, not a license. A lapsed policy is a gap in cover, and the insurer's reinstatement terms govern. Call the broker first; some will not backdate.
  • You are not sure which licenses you should hold. Start with your Secretary of State's site and the SBA's guide, which says to "research your own state, county, and city regulations" (SBA). This page is organizational guidance, not legal or tax advice.

Questions

Is there a grace period after a business license expires?

Only if the agency's own page says so, and the pages read for this one describe windows rather than grace periods. The CSLB accepts a written petition for retroactive renewal within 90 days of expiry, but the delinquent fee is still payable and work done in the gap is still unlicensed. Texas gives at least 45 days after a notice of pending forfeiture is mailed. Washington charges a late penalty on any renewal received after the expiration date. Treat a window as a deadline, not a cushion.

Can I keep operating while my business license is expired?

If the license is the permission to do the work, assume no: the CSLB says work performed while expired is unlicensed, and the EPA says a lapsed lead-safe firm may not conduct renovations until re-approved. If it is a registration or tax filing, the business usually keeps trading while penalties grow, and the risk is forfeiture or suspension once the notice period passes. Read your own agency's wording before deciding.

Does renewing late or reinstating erase the gap?

Not always. Texas lets a forfeited entity reinstate at any time with the missing reports, payment and a tax clearance letter (form 05-377), but the Comptroller's page says directors and officers are liable for the entity's debt during forfeiture. The CSLB lets you renew within five years, but the work in between was unlicensed. Keep the dates in the lapse record so you can answer the question later.

I never received a renewal reminder. Does that matter?

Not to the agency. Washington sends a courtesy reminder one month before expiry and says the renewal is due by the expiration date whether or not the notice arrives; Oregon's Secretary of State sends its notice about 45 days ahead (Oregon SOS). The date on the document is the obligation. Fix the address on file when you renew, and keep your own reminders.
