Home-based business license requirements: a checklist in the order to do it, and what renews

A business run from home usually needs the same city or county business license as a shop, plus permission to use a house for business. That permission is the zoning part: a home occupation permit, or a zoning check built into the license application. On top of that, add only what your work triggers. Selling goods needs a state seller's permit. Trading under a made-up name needs a DBA (fictitious business name) filing. A regulated trade needs its state license. Employees or an LLC need an EIN. Check your homeowners or renter's insurance too, because it rarely covers business liability. Do zoning first: it is the step that can say no.

The renewal trap: zoning is checked again

Most checklists stop once the license arrives. For a home business, the renewal is where things slip. Columbia, South Carolina, says all business licenses expire on April 30, renewal forms are mailed early in the year, and a home occupation must resubmit its home occupation form with the renewal "for re-validation by zoning". Paying late costs 5%, plus another 5% on the first of each month until paid. Not receiving the form is not a defense. A name or address change must be reported within ten days.

Los Angeles has a quieter version of the same trap. A business with $100,000 or less in gross receipts may qualify for the city's Small Business Exemption, but the Office of Finance must receive the renewal form on time to grant it. The renewal is due January 1 and delinquent on the first business day of March (LA Office of Finance FAQ). Miss the form and a business that owed nothing can owe penalties.

So the dates to put in a calendar the day each item arrives are: the business license expiry, any DBA expiry, the state license expiry, and the insurance renewal. Each one gets a reminder 30 days ahead, and the business license gets a note to fetch the zoning form too.

Worked example: candles sold from a Los Angeles apartment

Maya makes candles at home in Los Angeles. She sells them online, at weekend craft fairs, and to a few customers who collect from her door. She trades as "Maya Lane Candles".

  1. Lease. She checks her lease for a business-use clause before anything else.
  2. Zoning. Collections fit the city's limit of one client visit per hour between 8 am and 8 pm. She keeps no sign in the window.
  3. City. She registers for a Business Tax Registration Certificate, which the city says every business in Los Angeles needs. Her sales are well under $100,000, so she files the yearly renewal by January 1 to keep the Small Business Exemption.
  4. Seller's permit. California requires a seller's permit once you make three or more sales of merchandise in 12 months, from home or a store (CDTFA, Tax Guide for Home-Based Businesses). Her craft-fair sales are her own, so she needs one even though a marketplace collects tax on its own sales.
  5. DBA. "Maya Lane Candles" is not her legal name, so she files a fictitious business name statement with the Los Angeles County Clerk. Filed on 1 December 2026, it expires on 1 December 2031. Renewal costs $26 and needs no newspaper publication (LA County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk, Renewals).
  6. EIN. As a sole owner with no employees, she does not need one. The IRS requires an EIN if you have employees or operate an LLC, partnership or corporation (IRS, Employer identification number). She can still get one free for banking.
  7. Insurance. Her renter's policy is unlikely to cover a customer hurt at her door. She asks about a home business endorsement.

Her calendar then holds four renewal dates: the city business tax every January 1, the DBA on 1 December 2031, the renter's policy, and her sales tax return dates.

Insurance: the gap most home businesses miss

The Insurance Information Institute says a homeowners policy may cover business equipment only up to about $2,500 at home and $250 away from it, and usually does not cover business liability at all, such as a client who falls on your stairs (Triple-I, Insuring your home business). It describes three fixes: an endorsement on the home policy, separate business policies, or a small businessowners package. Ask your agent which fits.

When this page does not apply

  • You make food at home to sell. Most home occupation rules exclude it. Look up your state's cottage food rules; Chicago, for example, points to a Cottage Food Registration with its Department of Public Health.
  • You are a contractor working from home. Your state board's license matters most. See contractor license renewal dates and reminders.
  • You already have the license and need to renew it. See how to renew a business license.
  • A date has already passed. Read what happens if a business license expires.
  • You need the state's annual report date for an LLC. Find it in annual report due dates by state.

Questions

Do I need a business license if I only work online from home?

Usually yes. Cities and counties license the business, not the shopfront. Chicago, for example, says a City of Chicago business license is required to operate a business out of your home. What working online changes is the zoning side: with no visitors, deliveries or signs, a home office normally fits the home occupation rules easily.

Does my homeowners insurance cover my home business?

Often only a little. The Insurance Information Institute says a homeowners policy may cover business equipment only up to about $2,500 in the home and $250 away from it, and usually does not cover business liability at all. Ask your insurer about a home business endorsement or a separate policy.

Do I have to renew a home occupation permit?

It depends on the city. Chicago issues home occupation licenses for two years. In Columbia, South Carolina, every business license expires on April 30 and a home business must resubmit its home occupation form with each renewal so zoning can check it again. Put the renewal date in your calendar the day the license arrives.
